Matura Exam: Everything You Need to Know

📅 8 July 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ By Karolina

The Polish Matura exam is one of the most important milestones in a student's academic journey. For many, the English component can be particularly challenging.

But don't worry – with the right preparation, you can approach the Matura with confidence. This guide will break down everything you need to know.

📋 What Is the Matura Exam?

The Matura is the Polish secondary school leaving examination. The English exam tests your reading, writing, listening, and speaking skills at B1 or B2 level, depending on the level you choose.

The exam is divided into three parts:

  • Written exam – Reading, writing, and vocabulary
  • Listening exam – Comprehension of spoken English
  • Oral exam – Speaking with an examiner

📖 Reading Section

The reading section tests your ability to understand texts in English. You'll encounter different types of texts, including articles, emails, and short stories.

  • Time: Approximately 60-75 minutes
  • Question types: Multiple choice, true/false, matching, gap-fill
  • Focus: Main ideas, specific details, vocabulary, and understanding the author's purpose

💬 Tip: Read the questions before reading the text. This helps you know what to look for and saves time.

✍️ Writing Section

The writing section requires you to produce two pieces of writing:

  • Task 1: A short form text (email, postcard, or note)
  • Task 2: A longer essay on a given topic (opinion, argument, or description)

For the essay, structure is key. Use clear paragraphs, include an introduction and conclusion, and support your points with examples.

🎧 Listening Section

The listening section tests your ability to understand spoken English. You'll hear recordings of conversations, announcements, and monologues.

  • Time: Approximately 30-40 minutes
  • Tips: Practice listening to different accents (British and American)
  • Strategy: Read the questions before the recording starts

🗣️ Oral Exam

The oral exam is a conversation with an examiner. It's usually 10-15 minutes long and covers:

  • Part 1: General questions about yourself, family, and interests
  • Part 2: A short presentation on a given topic
  • Part 3: A conversation about the presentation topic

🎯 Key Tip: Speak clearly and confidently. Don't worry about making mistakes – the examiner wants to see how well you can communicate, not perfect English.

📈 Top 5 Tips for Success

  1. Start early: Begin preparing at least 3-4 months before the exam
  2. Practice every day: Even 20 minutes a day is better than one long session
  3. Use past papers: Familiarise yourself with the exam format and question types
  4. Expand your vocabulary: Learn new words daily and practice using them
  5. Take care of yourself: Get enough sleep, eat well, and stay calm on exam day
