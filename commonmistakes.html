10 Common Mistakes Polish Learners Make (And How to Fix Them)

📅 24 June 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ By Karolina

As a Polish learner of English, you face some unique challenges. Polish and English are very different languages, and some mistakes are more common than others.

The good news? Once you know what these mistakes are, you can fix them quickly. Here are the 10 most common mistakes I see and how to avoid them.

🇬🇧 1. The False Friends

❌ Mistake: "I need to eventually find a new job." (meaning "currently")

✅ Correction: "I need to actually find a new job."

"Eventually" means "in the end", not "currently". The Polish word "ewentualnie" means "possibly" – use "actually" for "currently".

🇬🇧 2. Missing Articles

❌ Mistake: "I bought car yesterday."

✅ Correction: "I bought a car yesterday."

Polish doesn't have articles, but English uses "a/an" and "the" constantly. Always check if you need an article!

🇬🇧 3. The "Do" and "Make" Confusion

❌ Mistake: "I need to make my homework."

✅ Correction: "I need to do my homework."

"Do" is for tasks and work. "Make" is for creating something new. "Make" a cake, "do" homework.

🇬🇧 4. Prepositions After Verbs

❌ Mistake: "I was waiting on my friend."

✅ Correction: "I was waiting for my friend."

Prepositions are tricky! "Wait for", "listen to", "look at". Learn verb + preposition combinations as phrases.

🇬🇧 5. Word Order in Questions

❌ Mistake: "You can help me?"

✅ Correction: "Can you help me?"

In English, questions invert the subject and auxiliary verb. "Can you...", "Are you...", "Do you..."

🇬🇧 6. The "What" and "Which" Confusion

❌ Mistake: "What colour do you prefer – blue or red?"

✅ Correction: "Which colour do you prefer – blue or red?"

Use "which" when there's a limited choice. Use "what" for open-ended questions.

🇬🇧 7. Verb Tenses – Present Simple vs Present Continuous

❌ Mistake: "I study for my exam right now."

✅ Correction: "I am studying for my exam right now."

Use Present Simple for habits and facts. Use Present Continuous for actions happening now or temporary situations.

🇬🇧 8. Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

❌ Mistake: "I need some informations."

✅ Correction: "I need some information."

"Information", "advice", "news" are uncountable in English. They don't take an 's' in the plural.

🇬🇧 9. The "In" and "On" Confusion for Time

❌ Mistake: "I will see you in Monday."

✅ Correction: "I will see you on Monday."

"On" for days, "in" for months and years. "At" for specific times. "On Monday", "In July", "At 3pm".

🇬🇧 10. Saying "How do you think?" Instead of "What do you think?"

❌ Mistake: "How do you think about this?"

✅ Correction: "What do you think about this?"

"How do you think?" sounds like you're asking about the process of thinking. Use "what" to ask for an opinion.

💬 My Advice: Don't try to fix all 10 mistakes at once. Pick one or two each week and focus on getting them right. Over time, they'll become automatic.

📈 How to Practice

  • Read daily: Notice how native speakers use these structures
  • Write regularly: Keep a journal in English
  • Listen carefully: Pay attention to patterns in podcasts and videos
  • Practice speaking: Mistakes are part of learning – the more you speak, the fewer mistakes you'll make
